TL;DR

Four decision areas, each with 3–5 genuine alternatives: (1) how many new categories and how they're derived from the 3 source files, (2) how to dedup concepts appearing in 2+ sources, (3) how FilterBar's chip UI should behave at 14–23 categories instead of 12, and (4) how the follow-up curation pass should be sequenced. Recommended package: source-structure-derived taxonomy (6 new categories, 18 total) + merge-into-richest-definition dedup + symmetric expand/collapse FilterBar fix (upgrading to pinned-chips-plus-dropdown if the category count ends up higher) + upfront dedup/mapping, then category-at-a-time curation. All four recommendations compose together, but each is presented with real alternatives for independent convergence.

Key Decisions

  • Taxonomy structure: recommend Alternative B (source-structure-derived, 6 new categories) over a 2-category consolidation or an 11-category maximal split.
  • Dedup strategy: recommend merging overlapping concepts into single richest-definition entries, not first-source-wins or duplicate-with-cross-reference.
  • FilterBar UX: recommend adding a genuine "show less" collapse as the minimum fix regardless of taxonomy outcome; upgrade to pinned-chips + dropdown only if the final count lands at the ~22–23 high end.
  • Curation plan: recommend resolving all cross-source dedup + category mapping up front, then curating category-by-category (not source-file-by-source-file).

Open Questions / Risks

  • Category-boundary judgment calls remain even under the recommended alternative (e.g. does "Bounded Contexts" belong under DDD or under Decision-Making & Modeling?) — convergence should confirm or overrule these explicitly.
  • Actual entry counts per new category are unknown until curation; sizing estimates in this document are illustrative, not commitments.
  • "Service discovery" has a real granularity mismatch across sources (1 concept vs. 5 named patterns) — the dedup table proposes a resolution, but it's a judgment call worth explicit sign-off.
  • The FilterBar recommendation is conditional on the taxonomy decision — confirm final category count before locking the UX alternative.

Decision Area 1 — New Category Taxonomy Structure

Baked into every alternative (constraint 3): content that clearly fits an existing category — CI/CD, IaC, monitoring, containers, Kubernetes, cloud deployment models (dna-mapa's "Infrastruktury"), and Kafka/Redis/Elasticsearch/Zookeeper/CDN (system-design terms.md) — enriches DevOps and Cloud Engineering in all three alternatives. What varies is how the genuinely new-territory content (DDD, architecture styles, distributed-systems theory, decision-making/modeling, ~35 microservices patterns) gets grouped.

Alt A — Minimal / Consolidated

14 total

Two umbrella categories: "Software Architecture" (DDD, arch styles, decision-making/modeling) and "Microservices & Distributed Systems" (dna-mapa's Systemowa + all ~35 microservice-patterns.md patterns, collapsing the source's own 3-way tag split).

Pros
  • Cheapest FilterBar impact (+2 chips)
  • Lowest boundary-judgment overhead & hand-sync risk
  • Neither category launches thin
Cons
  • Discards both sources' own intentional groupings
  • "Microservices & Distributed Systems" becomes the largest category, compressing signaled expertise
  • "Software Architecture" is internally heterogeneous (DDD + ADR + UML in one bucket)
Best when: FilterBar simplicity & minimal sync surface outweigh drill precision.

Alt B — Source-Structure-Derived

Recommended
18 total

6 new categories derived directly from each source's own structure: DDD & Architecture Styles, Architecture Decision-Making & Modeling, Distributed Systems Fundamentals, Microservices – Application Patterns, Microservices – Communication & Reliability, Microservices – Discovery & Deployment.

Pros
  • Nearly 1:1 preserves both sources' intentional structure
  • Category names read as credible/recognizable (serves Portfolio Visitor)
  • Sizing avoids both A's mega-category and C's thinness risk
Cons
  • Meaningfully raises FilterBar overflow pressure
  • Real boundary calls remain (e.g. Bounded Contexts placement)
  • 2 categories are the most likely to land thin after curation
Best when: drill-precision & source-fidelity matter more than minimizing chip count — true here since the UX redesign is explicitly in scope.

Alt C — Maximal / Granular

22–23 total

Splits Alt B further wherever the source supports it: 10–11 new categories (e.g. separating DDD tactical patterns from architecture styles, decision-making from visualization from strategic-DDD, communication from reliability/discovery).

Pros
  • Maximum topical precision for laser-focused drill sessions
  • Could read as confident taxonomy craftsmanship to a technical visitor who explores
Cons
  • Works against the project's "content maintenance over feature work" direction
  • Highest risk of new thin categories at launch (some ~3–6 entries)
  • Forces the most invasive FilterBar redesign; maximizes 3-way hand-sync drift risk
Best when: signaling maximal depth outweighs "uncluttered first impression."

Decision Area 2 — Cross-Source Overlap & Dedup Strategy

Known overlaps: CQRS, circuit breaker, service discovery, distributed tracing, Event Sourcing, Service Mesh / sidecar — each appears in 2+ of the 3 source files.

Alt 1 — First-Source-Wins

Fixed priority order per concept type (e.g. microservice-patterns.md wins for pattern names, dna-mapa wins for theory). The losing source is skimmed for additive material only.

Pros
  • Simple, fast, deterministic
  • Minimizes curation decision fatigue
Cons
  • Risks silently dropping valuable nuance
  • No answer for granularity mismatches (service discovery)
Best when: curation time is the binding constraint.

Alt 2 — Merge into Richest Definition

Recommended

Synthesize one entry per concept combining the best material from every source; when granularity mismatches, follow the more granular source's decomposition and fold the coarser source's context in as support.

Pros
  • Highest-quality entries, matches the app's synthesis-not-transcription philosophy
  • Protects Learn Mode's weighted drill from duplicate near-identical cards
Cons
  • Highest per-concept curation cost
  • Requires a real judgment call per overlap
Best when: entry quality & Learn Mode integrity outweigh curation-pass speed.

Alt 3 — Keep Separate + Cross-Reference

Allow two distinct entries per concept (one per category/angle) with a "see also" note in each description; no dedup.

Pros
  • Zero content loss
  • Preserves each source's own framing intact
Cons
  • Produces exactly the near-duplicate entries this task's own analysis warns against
  • Pollutes Learn Mode weighting; no UI affordance for the cross-reference
Best when: rarely — only for concepts whose two facets are too distinct to merge readably.

Decision Area 3 — FilterBar Chip/Overflow UX Redesign

Current mechanism: VISIBLE_CATEGORY_CHIP_COUNT = 5, a one-way showAllCategories boolean, fully generic renderChips() loop. Must remain usable at 14 (Alt A), 18 (Alt B), or 22–23 (Alt C) categories.

UX-1 — Raise Visible Count

Trivial cost

Bump VISIBLE_CATEGORY_CHIP_COUNT (5→8–10); mechanism otherwise unchanged.

Pros
  • Cheapest change, ships immediately
Cons
  • Doesn't solve "no re-collapse"
  • Insufficient alone at Alt C's count

UX-2 — Symmetric Collapse

Recommended
Small cost

Make showAllCategories genuinely bidirectional — add a "Show less" control that re-collapses the chip row.

Pros
  • Fixes the exact pain point flagged in analysis
  • No new grouping metadata, ~10-15 lines
Cons
  • Doesn't reduce chip count while expanded
  • Composes with UX-1, not a full fix alone at high counts

UX-3 — Two-Tier Clusters

Medium-high cost

Presentation-only theme clustering (e.g. "Microservices ▾" expands to 3 sub-chips); Category type stays flat.

Pros
  • Only alt that scales gracefully to 22–23 categories
  • Matches real study groupings
Cons
  • New 4th hand-synced artifact (grouping map)
  • New per-cluster state model, most new-code risk
  • Disproportionate to project's stated direction

UX-4 — Pinned Chips + Dropdown

Medium cost

Keep ~5 pinned chips, replace "+N more" with a dropdown/checkbox panel for the rest. Reuses existing toggleCategory logic.

Pros
  • Constant visual footprint regardless of category count
  • Best worst-case scalability below UX-3's cost
Cons
  • Loses always-visible live counts until opened
  • Bigger visual/interaction departure, needs design work

UX-5 — Search-Within-Filter

Small-medium cost

Type-ahead input that live-filters the chip set by substring match, independent of the main content search.

Pros
  • Cheap, purely additive, zero regression risk
  • Fast for the power-user Curator
Cons
  • Near-irrelevant to the Portfolio Visitor
  • Complement only, not a standalone fix

Decision Area 4 — Curation Plan Structure

Seq-1 — One Category at a Time

Fully curate (EN → PL → dedup check → validate) one category end-to-end before starting the next.

Pros
  • Incremental shippable checkpoints
  • Calibrates rubric mistakes early
Cons
  • Cross-source dedup harder without full concept visibility

Seq-2 — All Decisions First, Then Bulk-Curate

Finish 100% of taxonomy/dedup/mapping decisions before writing any entry, then curate in large batches.

Pros
  • Cleanest design/content separation
  • Best-quality dedup calls (full visibility)
Cons
  • No incremental checkpoint — risks the Curator's own named "curate everything at once" fatigue pattern

Seq-3 — Batch by Source File

microservice-patterns.md first (easiest), then system-design terms.md, then dna-mapa.md last (largest, Polish, highest risk).

Pros
  • Builds confidence before the hardest source
  • Cleanly isolates Polish-source discipline to one phase
Cons
  • Defers dna-mapa's contribution to every overlap, risking dedup retrofits
  • Categories look prematurely "done" after the English-source pass
Recommendation high confidence on sequencing medium on priority order Hybrid: resolve all cross-source dedup + category mapping decisions up front (this document + convergence — Seq-2's rigor), then curate category-by-category (Seq-1's cadence, not Seq-3's) to avoid retrofitting dedup decisions after the fact. Sequence categories by the Curator's actual near-term interview-prep priority. Move the Polish-source discipline (draft English first, then independently translate) into a permanent content-pipeline/rubric.md addition rather than a one-off note, so it's enforced on every future dna-mapa-sourced entry.
